## Preferred: Adobe Creative Cloud business-model transition

| ID | Official source | Period / locator | What it supports | Result status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ADBE-FY2012-10K-BASELINE` | [Adobe FY2012 Form 10-K, filed 22 Jan 2013 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634313000008/adbe10kfy12.htm) | Item 1, **Business — Digital Media / Creative Cloud**; Item 7, **Key performance metrics** and cash-flow statement | 2012 launch-stage subscription offering and the prior product/recurring-revenue context. It defines ARR and distinguishes it from revenue/deferred revenue; it is the factual baseline, not proof that a future Cloud-only choice would succeed. | Pre-main-decision context; result later exposed. |
| `ADBE-FY2013-10K-CHOICE-ADOPTION` | [Adobe FY2013 Form 10-K, filed 21 Jan 2014 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634314000004/adbe10kfy13.htm) | Item 1, **Business — Digital Media Strategy**; Item 7, **Key performance metrics / Results of Operations / Liquidity and Capital Resources** | Management states that in May 2013 it would deliver future creative-product innovations/features exclusively to Creative Cloud and that CS6 would be the last major perpetual-license update. It reports 1.4m paid subscriptions versus 0.3m at FY2012 end and Creative ARR $768m versus $155m, and provides company-level operating cash flow. | Official known early outcome, exposed. |
| `ADBE-FY2014-10K-SCALE-CASH` | [Adobe FY2014 Form 10-K, filed 20 Jan 2015 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634315000022/adbe10kfy14.htm) | Item 7, **Financial Performance Summary**, **Key performance metrics**, **Results of Operations** and **Liquidity and Capital Resources** | Later adoption/economic readout: 3.454m paid subscriptions, Creative ARR $1.68bn, subscription revenue 50% of total revenue, and consolidated operating cash flow $1.288bn. It also repeats the needed warning that ARR is independent of recognized/deferred revenue. | Official known later outcome, exposed. |

### Preferred-source use guardrails

- “Paid subscription” documents a paid, current subscriber count under Adobe's disclosed definition. It is not a cohort-retention, gross-margin, price-elasticity or CAC measure.
- Creative ARR is a management-defined annualized performance metric. The FY2013/FY2014 filings expressly say to view it independently of revenue, deferred revenue and unbilled deferred revenue; those measures must never be arithmetically merged.
- Company operating cash flow includes Digital Marketing and other Adobe operations. It supports no Creative Cloud-alone owner-cash conclusion.
- The filings make a defensible within-case sequence—choice, product/price/channel changes, adoption, reporting transition and aggregate cash resilience—but not a causal claim that the pricing decision alone produced every result or that the perpetual alternative would have failed.

### Backup-source use guardrails

- The fact that JCP's management says the strategy did not resonate with customers supports a strong within-company lesson; it still does not isolate pricing from assortment, presentation, leadership, macro conditions or online competition.
- Traffic, conversion, transaction and sales changes are customer/operating evidence. They do not by themselves measure all competitor responses or prove that every clearance dollar was caused by the pricing choice.
- Free cash flow and debt figures in these filings are company-level outcomes. Do not call them the direct cash cost of Fair and Square without a responsibility-matched bridge.
- Neither the backup selection nor its later result grants a valuation, security-return or general management-quality verdict.
