## Why this fills the gap

Primary curriculum role: **`PERMANENT_LOSS_AND_LIFECYCLE`**, with a material
secondary role in **`MANAGEMENT_DECISION_EXECUTION`**.

The decision was unusually explicit. In September 2013, after operating a
partnership announced in 2011, Microsoft elected to purchase substantially all
of Nokia's Devices & Services business and end the related joint strategic
initiatives at closing. The real management choice is therefore an ownership
and operating-model change: acquire the phone-design/manufacturing/sales
perimeter and integrate it, versus retain/rework the existing partner route.
The latter is a factual pre-existing baseline, not a claim that continuation
would have been costless or superior.

The exposed result permits a complete but deliberately non-binary teaching
chain:

```text
partner route -> acquire / internalize phone hardware -> integrate people,
facilities and operating model -> phone volume, revenue goal and margin-mix
tests -> strategy narrowing -> asset write-down + further restructuring
```

The FY2015 filing records sold handset volumes, but also says the business did
not meet its sales-volume and revenue goals, that mix carried lower margins
than planned, and that lower expected future cash flows followed a strategy
shift. Microsoft then recorded $7.5bn of Phone Hardware goodwill/asset
impairments. This is not a "success became failure" story: sales volume,
revenue, margin mix, integration implementation, accounting impairment and
cash commitment are separate clocks. The teaching point is that an
implemented acquisition with observable customer shipments is not equivalent
to a recoverable capital commitment.

The lifecycle/decision-execution mechanism is heterogeneous to the local
capacity, pricing, product, channel-credit and standalone turnaround cases:
it converts a pre-existing strategic partner into an owned global hardware
system, then tests whether post-integration customer volume and mix support
the expected future cash flows. It also differs from treating an impairment
as a complete cash-loss measure: the acquisition's $7.1bn cash component is
an actual capital deployment, whereas the subsequent impairment is an
accounting recognition of reduced recoverability. They must not be added or
called the same loss.

## Minimum teaching question and boundary

At the 2013–14 decision point, what observable evidence would be required to
prefer internalizing NDS over retaining/reworking the existing alliance?

1. **Execution:** transfer and integrate the production, sales, marketing and
   support perimeter without treating a headcount plan as integration success.
2. **Customer/economic path:** demonstrate that handset volume and the unit
   mix can satisfy the business's own revenue and profitability requirements;
   shipments alone are insufficient.
3. **Cash/capital path:** distinguish the cash acquisition component from
   non-cash impairment and from restructuring cash/other charges; do not infer
   a deal IRR, security return, valuation, or an all-in management-quality
   verdict.

Known-result resolution is limited to the disclosed chain. It supports the
boundary that the acquired Phone Hardware carrying value became unrecoverable
under Microsoft's FY2015 assumptions after the stated operating shortfall and
strategy shift. It **does not** prove that every impairment dollar was an
incremental cash loss, that the alliance alternative would have won, or that
Microsoft's broader mobile/cloud strategy did or did not succeed.
