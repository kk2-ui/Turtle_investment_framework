# Teaching case draft — China Overseas Property (HK:02669)

## Third-party growth: when does managed area become cash an owner can actually claim?

**Candidate:** `TCAND:HK02669`  
**Episode window:** 31 December 2022 to 31 December 2023; the outcome is known.  
**Purpose:** teach the distinction between winning/adding managed gross floor area (GFA), creating durable customer absorption and project-level economics, collecting the consideration, and producing owner cash.  
**Not in scope:** valuation, a buy price, CJO, or an investment recommendation.

### Case boundary

This is deliberately a one-year, post-outcome episode. The opening state is the FY2022 portfolio; the management choice and operating results are FY2023; the disclosed cash and credit results are FY2023. 2023 changed the presentation currency to Renminbi and restated comparatives. Financial comparisons below therefore use the FY2023 annual report's **RMB-restated** comparatives. The FY2022 report is used for the pre-decision portfolio state, principally in square metres and percentages, not to splice its HK$ financials into the FY2023 figures.

The known result is mixed: third-party expansion and property-management profit did occur, but the official disclosure does not identify third-party renewal, customer-satisfaction outcomes, third-party cohort unit economics, or the source/sustainability of owner-distributable cash. A good answer must not promote the former into proof of the latter.

## The economic episode

### 1. State and constraint at the decision boundary

At 31 December 2022, China Overseas Property (COPL) managed 320.3m sq.m. of GFA. Independent third parties represented 105.2m sq.m., or 32.8%, up from 71.6m sq.m. (27.6%) a year earlier. The portfolio was also moving toward lump-sum contracts: 248.3m sq.m. (77.5%) was lump sum and 72.0m sq.m. (22.5%) commission basis. [S1, printed pp. 30–33]

The economic constraint was not merely the availability of projects. In a lump-sum contract COPL is the principal, recognises the management fee receivable as revenue and bears the related service costs; it therefore carries the cost and receivable exposure. Commission-basis revenue is reported very differently, so its 100% reported gross margin is not evidence that a comparable amount of cash profit is being generated. [S2, printed pp. 172, 177]

For the case decision, management faced a real trade-off:

* pursue more independent third-party projects and accept the need to execute service quality, price, staffing/subcontracting and collections across a more heterogeneous portfolio;
* stay more selective—expand only where early project performance, fee collection and renewal evidence were already visible; or
* prioritise cash collection and existing-project retention before adding scale.

The second and third bullets are analytical alternatives, not a claim that the board formally tabled them. The disclosed choice was the first: COPL says it proactively expanded through more projects from independent third parties, using its brand equity and scale advantage. In FY2022 it described the broader expansion programme as partly via equity investment and partly by third-party wins. [S1, printed pp. 26, 30]

### 2. The management action and the observed customer/operating reaction

In FY2023, 57.3m sq.m.—70.6% of the 81.2m sq.m. of new orders—came from independent third parties, with disclosed contract sums of RMB4,002.2m. Total managed GFA rose 25.4% to 401.5m sq.m.; third-party GFA ended at 162.5m sq.m., 40.5% of the portfolio, versus 105.2m sq.m. and 32.8% at FY2022 year end. [S2, printed pp. 35, 39]

That is an observable market response: customers awarded contracts and COPL added GFA. Management also states that improved service quality and customer satisfaction strengthened its brand recognition. But that sentence is management narrative, not a disclosed satisfaction score, renewal rate, termination rate, referral rate, or third-party project-quality data. The annual report does not connect a named independent-project cohort to subsequent renewal or collections. Thus the case can say **new customer wins occurred**; it cannot say that the FY2023 third-party expansion had been absorbed through customer satisfaction or renewal.

The operating result was better than a pure-area story, but still not attributable to independent projects. Property-management revenue rose 16.0% to RMB9,414.9m (the report says core property-management revenue rose 25.7%, in line with GFA). Lump-sum revenue rose 16.8% to RMB9,177.2m, and its reported gross margin improved from 10.6% to 12.7%; total property-management segment gross margin rose from 13.4% to 15.0%, with segment profit up 35.0% to RMB1,320.2m. Management attributes the lump-sum margin improvement to a streamlined human-resource structure, more subcontracting and the absence of 2022 COVID-prevention costs. [S2, printed pp. 37–42]

This is a plausible operational improvement, but not proof that a newly won third-party square metre was profitable. The report does not cross-tab revenue, margin, subcontracting cost, service failures, or retention by project source (independent versus group-related) or by the 2023 new-order cohort. The RMB4,002.2m contract sum also lacks disclosed contract duration and service scope, so it is not an annual fee yield per sq.m. and cannot be used as unit economics.

### 3. Cash, capital and permanent-loss outcome

Cash followed the growth, but only at the consolidated-group level. FY2023 operating cash flow before working-capital changes was RMB1,859.2m. The cash-flow statement records a RMB657.5m increase in trade receivables, retention receivables and other contract assets, plus a RMB140.4m increase in prepayments, deposits and other receivables. Those uses were offset in part by RMB652.9m more trade payables and RMB267.8m more receipts in advance/other deposits; net operating cash flow was RMB1,504.7m. [S2, printed p. 140]

There was also an observed cash transfer to ordinary shareholders at the group level: the FY2023 cash-flow statement records RMB407.854m of dividends paid to shareholders of the Company. Note 13 identifies this as the RMB240.427m FY2022 final dividend plus the RMB167.427m FY2023 interim dividend. By contrast, the approximately RMB256.907m FY2023 final dividend was only proposed at the reporting date, subject to shareholder approval, and was not reflected as a dividend payable in the FY2023 financial statements; it must not be described as cash paid in FY2023. [S2, printed pp. 141, 196]

The credit ledger is directionally uncomfortable rather than conclusive. Gross trade receivables rose to RMB2,620.5m from RMB1,999.7m; the over-two-year bucket rose to RMB214.4m from RMB142.1m. The loss allowance rose to RMB170.2m from RMB133.4m, and the note records RMB28.5m of receivables written off in 2023 (FY2022: RMB11.3m). The same note says trade receivables arise from lump-sum property management, value-added services and parking-space trading, and gives no source-of-project or vintage split. [S2, printed pp. 207–210]

Consequently:

* Positive **group** operating cash flow is real, but it is not third-party cohort collection or sustainable cash after maintenance and growth needs.
* `GROUP_LEVEL_ORDINARY_SHAREHOLDER_CASH_TRANSFER = OBSERVED`: RMB407.854m was actually paid to the Company's shareholders in FY2023. It is an observed financing cash outflow, not evidence that the FY2023 independent-third-party cohort funded it or that the transfer is repeatable.
* `THIRD_PARTY_COHORT_OWNER_CASH = UNKNOWN`: the disclosure does not link those dividends, CFO, collections, receivables, or write-offs to third-party projects or to the FY2023 new-order cohort.
* `SUSTAINABLE_MAINTENANCE_ADJUSTED_OWNER_CASH = UNKNOWN`: the disclosure does not separate maintenance from expansion cash needs or establish recurring distributable capacity. The paid dividend and the proposed FY2023 final dividend do not close that gap.
* The RMB28.5m write-off is an observed accounting derecognition in the group receivable book; it is the closest disclosed realised credit-loss datum. It is **not** evidence that the third-party expansion created RMB28.5m of permanent loss, nor that it did not: allocation to third-party projects and to the FY2023 cohort is absent.

## Evidence ladder: what each fact can and cannot carry

| Official evidence | What it establishes | What it does **not** establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 57.3m sq.m. independent-party new orders; third-party GFA 40.5% at year end [S2 pp. 35, 39] | Contract wins and a larger third-party managed-area footprint | Customer retention, satisfaction, realised project quality, price quality, or cash collection |
| Property-management revenue and reported contract-basis margins [S2 pp. 37, 41–42] | Consolidated segment revenue and reported economics by **contract basis** | Margin or fee yield of third-party projects, the new-order cohort, or a renewal cohort |
| Management's service-quality/customer-satisfaction statement [S2 p. 39] | Management's stated mechanism and brand claim | An independently measured satisfaction/renewal outcome or causal proof for external wins |
| RMB1,504.7m operating cash flow [S2 p. 140] | Consolidated cash-flow outcome for the year | Third-party collection or sustainable, maintenance-adjusted owner cash |
| RMB407.854m dividends paid to shareholders of the Company: RMB240.427m FY2022 final plus RMB167.427m FY2023 interim [S2 pp. 141, 196] | An actual group-level ordinary-shareholder cash transfer in FY2023 | Funding by third-party projects, third-party cohort owner cash, or sustainable free cash flow; the RMB256.907m FY2023 final dividend was proposed, not paid in FY2023 |
| Receivable ageing, ECL allowance and write-offs [S2 pp. 207–210] | Consolidated credit exposure and realised accounting write-offs | Loss rate, recovery, or permanent-loss exposure of third-party projects specifically |

## The strongest counter-case

The bearish mechanism is not that expansion failed—the contract wins, GFA gain and property-management profit increase are disclosed. It is that the incremental footprint may be lower-quality or more cash-intensive than the aggregate result implies.

In particular, the apparent margin recovery is explainable without attributing any benefit to third-party expansion: management points to staffing/subcontracting efficiency and the disappearance of COVID costs. At the same time, the receivable book and over-two-year balance grew, with a larger write-off. A third-party project could therefore add managed area and recognised revenue while pricing remains competitive, payment behaviour weakens, or renewal costs rise. The evidence does not prove that this happened to the 2023 external cohort; it prevents the opposite inference as well.

The counter-case is strongest because the cash-flow statement also contains material offsets from higher trade payables and advance receipts. Treating its RMB1.5bn CFO as sustainable owner cash would hide the growth in receivables and would improperly convert a group cash-flow presentation into a third-party cohort conclusion. The RMB407.854m shareholder dividend proves an actual group-level cash transfer, but does not resolve either the cohort-attribution or maintenance-adjusted-sustainability gap.

## Near miss: the temporary Hong Kong isolation-facility revenue

The FY2023 report says property-management revenue growth was restrained by fewer additional management revenue after the decommissioning of Hong Kong community isolation facilities early in the year. [S2, printed p. 40]

This can look superficially like the same mechanism—more managed activity producing property-management revenue—but it is a different one: a temporary facility-management assignment, not evidence that an independent third-party portfolio has retained COPL due to customer satisfaction, achieved recurring unit economics, or converted fees into collection-backed owner cash. It should be excluded from any renewal/absorption inference.

## Best-current investor handling after the outcome

For research classification only, the defensible status is:

* `THIRD_PARTY_SCALE = OBSERVED` — third-party wins and ending GFA share are official facts.
* `OPERATING_ECONOMICS = PARTIALLY_OBSERVED_AT_GROUP_CONTRACT_BASIS` — the reported lump-sum and segment margins improved, but source/cohort economics are unobserved.
* `CUSTOMER_ABSORPTION_OR_RENEWAL = UNKNOWN` — no renewal, termination, satisfaction-score or project-quality evidence is disclosed for the external cohort.
* `COLLECTION_AND_PERMANENT_LOSS_BY_THIRD_PARTY_COHORT = UNKNOWN` — group receivable deterioration and write-offs are observed, but not allocable.
* `GROUP_LEVEL_ORDINARY_SHAREHOLDER_CASH_TRANSFER = OBSERVED` — RMB407.854m was paid in FY2023 (FY2022 final RMB240.427m and FY2023 interim RMB167.427m); the FY2023 final RMB256.907m was proposed, not paid in the FY2023 statements.
* `THIRD_PARTY_COHORT_OWNER_CASH = UNKNOWN` — no linkage from third-party project cash collection to that shareholder transfer is disclosed.
* `SUSTAINABLE_MAINTENANCE_ADJUSTED_OWNER_CASH = UNKNOWN` — maintenance versus growth needs and recurring distributable capacity are not separated.

Accordingly, a best-current investor should record the shareholder cash transfer as observed while keeping third-party expansion as a **growth fact with collection and owner-cash gates**, not crediting it as a completed quality-to-cash loop. The appropriate next research action is to seek source/cohort evidence; this statement is not a valuation, purchase, sale, or portfolio instruction.

## Local UNKNOWNs that remain material

1. Independent-third-party renewal, termination and re-tender rates, including the age and contract term of the FY2023 new-order cohort.
2. Customer-satisfaction scores, complaints, service-level performance and project-quality outcomes, split between third-party and group-related projects.
3. Fee level, annualised revenue, direct labour/subcontracting cost, gross margin and incremental overhead per third-party sq.m.
4. Invoice collection, ageing, ECL, recovery and write-off by customer type, project source and service line.
5. The source of the observed RMB407.854m group-level shareholder cash transfer in the FY2023 third-party cohort; maintenance cash versus expansion cash; and recurring cash available to ordinary owners after those needs and any upstream/legal constraints.

## Transfer question — verify this first at another company

Before transferring the apparent lesson to another property manager, first ask: **does that company separately disclose (1) actual group-level ordinary-shareholder cash transfers, (2) a third-party project cohort from award through renewal/termination, annualised fee and direct cost, invoice collection/ageing/write-off, and (3) the bridge from that cohort cash to sustainable, maintenance-adjusted owner-available cash?** If it cannot, managed-area growth should remain a scale observation rather than evidence of durable customer absorption or third-party cohort owner cash.

## Teaching prompts

1. At the FY2022 boundary, which information would have made the selective-expansion alternative economically testable rather than merely cautious?
2. In FY2023, which observed facts support an operating improvement, and which facts are only scale or accounting presentation?
3. The RMB407.854m shareholder dividend is an observed cash transfer. What further disclosure would link it, if at all, to the FY2023 third-party cohort and separately establish sustainable, maintenance-adjusted owner cash? Why are aggregate CFO and the proposed RMB256.907m FY2023 final dividend insufficient?
4. Would the rise in over-two-year receivables change your view of third-party expansion without a source-of-project split? State both the concern and the prohibited inference.
