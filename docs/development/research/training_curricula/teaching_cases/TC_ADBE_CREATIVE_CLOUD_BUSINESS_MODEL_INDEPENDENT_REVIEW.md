# TC-ADBE Creative Cloud business-model — independent review

**Verdict: ACCEPT**  
**Path:** `ACCEPT / source-bounded teaching draft is fit for the stated result-known, teaching-only use.`

## Scope and evidence basis

This review was limited to the two Adobe candidate/source-inventory documents, the three listed Adobe FY2012–FY2014 SEC Forms 10-K, and the submitted draft. It does not assess later results, valuation, security returns, analyst views, or other teaching material.

## Material source and inference review

The central factual chain is supported by the permitted primary sources. The FY2013 10-K states the May 2013 choice to deliver new creative-product innovations and features exclusively to Creative Cloud subscribers, identifies CS6 as the last major perpetual-license update, and still notes that CS6 remained available on a perpetual basis. The FY2014 10-K records the later removal of general CS6 availability from legacy shrinkwrap and volume channels, the remaining Adobe.com/certain-market availability, and immaterial fourth-quarter perpetual creative-professional revenue. The draft accurately treats those as management/action facts, rather than claiming an overnight end to all perpetual sales.

The measured resolution is likewise sound:

- Paid Creative Cloud subscriptions, Creative ARR, total Adobe subscription revenue, consolidated deferred revenue, and consolidated operating cash flow are separately named and kept on their disclosed responsibility boundaries.
- The FY2013 and FY2014 filings expressly instruct readers to view ARR independently of revenue, deferred revenue, and unbilled deferred revenue. The draft follows that instruction and flags the FY2013 $768m versus FY2014-presented $801m comparator/definition issue rather than manufacturing a precise cross-presentation ARR growth rate.
- The reported paid-subscription, ARR, subscription-revenue-mix, deferred-revenue, and operating-cash figures used in the draft agree in substance with the three filings. The revenue and deferred-revenue descriptions preserve their consolidated/other-business content; the operating-cash discussion is expressly company-level.

The resulting judgment is positive but properly bounded: the filings support that the transition had real current paid adoption and a meaningful recurring-revenue migration by FY2014, and that aggregate operating cash generation did not collapse through the transition. They do not support the stronger claims that the draft refuses to make: original-cohort retention, CAC/payback, Creative-Cloud-only cost to serve or maintenance capital, Creative-Cloud-alone owner cash, or a causal proof that removal of perpetual updates alone produced the observed outcome. This is a decision-useful teaching conclusion, not a defensive whole-case `UNKNOWN`.

## Management/no-action and scope boundaries

The factual no-action baseline is correctly limited to the existing perpetual-license/upgrade route. The document marks the partial-migration path as an analytical, unobserved alternative and does not assign it an invented outcome. Its decision- and execution-quality statements are explicitly confined to the business-model switch; it disclaims any Adobe-wide management, moat, capital-allocation, valuation, buy/return, or general management-quality conclusion.

The draft does not overshoot into cohort retention, CAC, standalone product cash, company-wide management, or valuation. Its scenario and permanent-loss language retains those missing bridges as local discriminating conditions, rather than converting absence of disclosure into either failure or proven owner-cash durability.

No material data-coverage, acquisition-module, reasoning, model, or writing defect was identified within the requested review scope.
