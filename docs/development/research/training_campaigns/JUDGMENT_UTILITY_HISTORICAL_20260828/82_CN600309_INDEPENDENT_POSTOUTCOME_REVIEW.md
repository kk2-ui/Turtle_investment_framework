# CN600309 万华化学 FY2024 post-outcome independent review

**Verdict: ACCEPT.**  FY2024 supports the frozen enterprise judgment: capacity deployment and revenue growth did not earn automatic credit as normal earnings, owner cash, or capital return.  The result is materially adverse for the two operating axes, but it is not evidence of an immediate cash or refinancing crisis.  This is a market-free enterprise readout, not a valuation or investment-action conclusion.

## Investor readout

### A — core polyurethane absorption and spread: NEGATIVE, with a bounded meaning

The frozen first-match rule settles A as `NEGATIVE`: polyurethane-project utilization was **88%**, below the 90% adverse threshold (FY2024 annual report p.26).  This specifically refutes giving the enlarged polyurethane asset base automatic normal-earnings credit.  The pre-outcome question was whether new assets would be economically absorbed, rather than whether they could be commissioned; FY2024 does not clear that test.

The negative label must not be narrated as a collapse of the legacy polyurethane franchise.  The same report records 580万吨 production, 564万吨 sales, and 57万吨 inventory; production and sales rose 16.22% and 15.40%, respectively, while inventory rose 38.50%.  Polyurethane revenue rose 12.55% and its gross margin remained 26.15%, albeit down 1.54 percentage points (p.17).  Management also reports record MDI/TDI/polyether/modified-product volumes and shares, and says it completed the Fujian and Ningbo MDI expansions (pp.9, 26).  These are meaningful recovery facts: the mature franchise still appears to have broad customer demand and a high absolute margin.  They do not overturn the frozen settlement because utilization fell below its adverse boundary and the report does not separate legacy demand, incremental-capacity ramp, or their respective realized spreads.

For normal earnings, the right update is therefore to withhold incremental-capacity credit, not to erase the established core.  Group attributable profit was RMB130.33bn, down 22.49% from FY2023 (p.7), below the pre-outcome RMB140–190bn observation range; that outcome reinforces caution on group normal earnings, but one annual result is not proof that the core's normalized profitability is permanently RMB130.33bn.  A does not independently measure owner cash, so it cannot be converted into a liquidity conclusion.  Its permanent-loss relevance is conditional: persistently under-utilized new chemical assets would lower incremental returns and eventually impair reinvestment economics; the observed sales growth and still-high core margin mean the annual report does not establish that permanence today.

### B — petrochemical and new-materials unit economics: NEGATIVE

B is also correctly `NEGATIVE`: fine chemicals and new materials produced a 12.78% gross margin, below the 15% frozen adverse threshold.  Revenue in that series grew 18.61%, but cost grew 31.66%, reducing margin by 8.64 percentage points (p.17).  Petrochemicals remained a 3.52% gross-margin business, essentially unchanged from 3.51% (p.17).  The outcome thus directly supports the frozen distinction between deployment/revenue and economic profit: neither the higher non-core revenue nor project/commercialization descriptions should be put into normal earnings as if they were a proven profit pool.

This does not say every non-core business is loss-making or devoid of customer traction.  The report describes several products as gaining share or entering customer applications (pp.9, 23–24), and petrochemical margin was modestly positive.  But the disclosed aggregate margin shows that those operating facts have not yet translated into an adequate aggregate new-materials unit return.  The owner-cash implication is indirect but material: cash committed to a lower-margin expansion pool cannot be treated as distributable owner cash merely because sales grew.  The permanent-loss mechanism remains the pre-specified one—continued investment into assets that earn persistently inadequate spreads—not an asserted current solvency event.

### C — growth-capex cash conversion and debt load: MIXED, and not a cure for A or B

C settles `MIXED` under the frozen rule.  FY2024 operating cash flow was RMB30.053bn and cash paid for long-lived assets was RMB35.830bn, a **0.8388** coverage ratio; the cash deficit was therefore about RMB5.777bn (p.87).  The frozen short-term-borrowings/current-portion/long-term-borrowings total was RMB94.915bn, only **3.31%** above the FY2023 frozen base (p.80).  Cash flow improved 12.15% year over year, capex cash spending declined from RMB43.098bn, and net financing cash inflow fell sharply from RMB22.809bn to RMB6.186bn (pp.7, 16, 87).

The investor meaning is deliberately two-sided.  Total long-lived-asset cash spending still exceeded operating cash, so owner cash is not demonstrated by the report's total-capex measure; the maintenance-versus-growth split remains unavailable.  Conversely, the much smaller cash gap and contained frozen-debt increase are evidence against calling FY2024 an immediate financing or cash crisis.  C's `MIXED` status does **not** offset A's failure to earn incremental polyurethane absorption credit or B's demonstrated non-core margin deterioration.  It simply localizes the capital-risk update: expansion remains cash-consuming, while the balance-sheet burden did not worsen enough in FY2024 to establish acute funding stress.

## Strongest countercase, recovery facts, and still-unsettled local questions

The strongest countercase is that a 26.15% polyurethane margin, expanding volumes, record reported market shares, and a much narrower aggregate cash shortfall show that the company is successfully ramping a durable integrated core through a weak chemical cycle; its investment program could yet compound normal earnings.  That case is credible and is why A's 88% utilization is not evidence that existing polyurethane economics have failed, and why C is not negative.  It is presently weaker than the cautious base treatment because the additional asset base has not met the frozen utilization test, non-core revenue growth coincided with a large aggregate margin reset, and total capex still exceeds operating cash.

The annual report leaves material but localized questions unresolved:

- It does not separate legacy versus newly added polyurethane capacity, volume, inventory, realized price, margin, or cash return.  Average utilization therefore cannot identify whether the 88% result is a temporary ramp, demand shortfall, or both.
- Fine chemicals and new materials are reported as an aggregate.  The filing supports a negative aggregate unit-economics result, but not a product-level conclusion about POE, battery materials, PC, or other individual projects.
- Cash spending is not split between maintenance and growth capital, and project-level cash returns are absent.  Total-capex cash coverage is a valid owner-cash constraint, not a precise distributable-cash estimate.

These gaps require neither an invented causal attribution nor a whole-case refusal.  They bound the claims above.

## Next unseen-case transfer assessment

**`NO_MATERIAL_NEW_METHOD_UTILITY` — not `TRANSFER_CANDIDATE`.**  This result is a useful confirmation of the frozen judgment, but not a demonstrated new method lesson.  Before FY2024 was opened, the three axes had already required exactly the research behavior that this outcome rewards:

- A required utilization together with production, sales, inventory, and margin, and explicitly barred capacity announcements as substitutes.
- B required both non-core gross margins and explicitly barred revenue, volumes, capacity, and project progress as substitutes.
- C required operating cash versus long-lived-asset cash spending plus a fixed debt scope, and explicitly barred net income and project completion as substitutes.

The fact that FY2024 hit A and B's adverse cells and C's mixed cell does not, by itself, create a new pre-outcome field or research behavior for a future unknown case.  Claiming transfer credit here would confuse a successful application of already-frozen discipline with new learning.

**Next-case agenda delta: `NONE`.**  Retain the existing three-axis agenda and its responsibility boundaries.  A later revision would need evidence that an existing field or behavior failed to distinguish a material investor outcome; this single settlement does not supply that evidence.
