# CN600309 pre-outcome independent review

**Verdict: ACCEPT.** The frozen forecast can be handed to an independent custodian before the FY2024 source is unsealed.

This is an investor-useful, falsifiable enterprise episode rather than a defensive field inventory.  Its central judgment is appropriately conditional: the FY2023 polyurethane franchise has demonstrated absorption and cost resilience, but project commissioning is not credited as earnings, owner cash, or capital return until absorption, spread, and cash/debt evidence each arrive.  That conclusion separates capacity/deployment from sales, customer absorption, unit economics, cash conversion, and return on incremental capital.

The three settlement axes have real, non-overlapping responsibility boundaries and can be mechanically settled from an annual report.  Axis A is confined to polyurethane utilization, sales versus production, and gross margin; Axis B to the two non-core segment margins; Axis C to operating cash relative to long-lived-asset cash spending and a frozen debt scope.  The rules neither let group profit, project completion, capacity announcements, nor one axis's result substitute for another.  Their asymmetric design is appropriate: full evidence is needed for a positive conclusion, while a materially adverse disclosed indicator can settle a negative conclusion; incomplete disclosure is localized as `LOCAL_UNKNOWN`.

The material starting facts and thresholds are supported by the FY2023 annual report: FY2023 revenue/profit/cash flow (p.7), product revenue, margins, production/sales/inventory and price/cost context (pp.16-18), concentration and working-capital context (pp.19, 72-73, 79-80), projects and business economics (pp.9-13, 23-26), utilization (p.25), and the balance-sheet debt figures (pp.72-73).  The operating-cash, long-lived-asset-spend, investment-cash-flow, and financing-cash-flow figures are displayed across pp.79-80; this page break does not weaken the evidence or settlement rule.

The countercase is genuine: high polyurethane utilization, volume growth, margin improvement, and demonstrated project execution could mean the expansion compounds an already resilient advantage.  The base case answers it economically rather than dismissing it: weak overseas conditions, a 3.51% petrochemical margin, declining new-material margin, and externally supported investment cash needs mean that execution milestones alone cannot earn incremental owner-cash or capital-return credit.  The bull/base/bear branches, reversal observations, permanent-loss mechanism, and market-free valuation direction all preserve that distinction.  The owner-cash limitation is correctly local: maintenance versus growth capex is not invented, while the observed total-capex cash burden remains decision-relevant.

No price, return, formal valuation, BuyBand, or investment action is embedded in the judgment.
