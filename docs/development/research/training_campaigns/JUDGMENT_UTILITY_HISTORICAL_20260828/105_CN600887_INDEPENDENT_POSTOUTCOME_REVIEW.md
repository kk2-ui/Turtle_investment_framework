# CN600887 Round 8 Post-outcome Review

**Verdict: ACCEPT.** The FY2024 settlement matches the frozen contract and the authorized annual-report locations. It changes the enterprise judgment materially, but reveals no defect that warrants a return of the settlement or a rewrite of the historical pre-outcome contract.

The conditional-positive pre-outcome judgment is **materially weakened**, not wholly overturned. The forecast correctly kept customer absorption, product margin, owner cash, and capital return separate: FY2024 delivers a genuine cash success and two product-margin improvements while simultaneously failing the broader demand test and the capital-return test. A conclusion that calls the company either “cash-proven” or “economically broken” would discard this separation.

The most important pre-outcome claim that is **overturned** is J1: adjacent categories did not compensate for core liquid-milk pressure on the defined sales-volume evidence, because both liquid milk and cold drinks fell below their FY2023 baselines. J2 is **partly confirmed**: mix and execution were not shown to be universally margin accretive, even though two product legs improved. J3 is **split**: the cash-repeatability concern is weakened by A3, but the capital-return concern is strengthened by the 15.81% ROE result. The prior view that improvement had not yet demonstrated stable, repeatable capital returns therefore remains conservative in the right direction and becomes more important, rather than being validated as an undifferentiated negative.

## Management credit and the revised permanent-loss view

Management retains limited execution credit for protecting/improving liquid-milk and powder/dairy gross margins and for generating the defined cash proxy after lower recorded long-term-asset cash spend. It loses outcome credit for the product/channel program as a broad customer-absorption answer: core liquid-milk volume fell, and cold-drink volume fell materially. It also loses enterprise-level capital-allocation credit because ROE fell below the prior low point. These are responsibility-matched outcome credits, not causal proof that any named channel, innovation, supply-chain, or overseas project individually caused the result.

Customer absorption has weakened. Unit economics are mixed rather than broadly impaired. Owner cash is stronger on the frozen enterprise proxy. Capital return has weakened materially. Taken together, permanent-loss risk rises from the prior **medium** assessment to **medium-high conditional on a demand/ROE recovery**: resilient margins and cash capacity limit the case for an immediate high-risk conclusion, but shrinking core-and-cold-drink customer absorption together with sub-FY2022 ROE means a brand, scale, and cash-flow narrative no longer protects the capital-return thesis.

The strongest current counterargument is that the volume declines are a one-year demand or channel-reset episode, while the 0.62-point liquid-milk and 2.88-point powder/dairy margin improvements plus strong cash proxy demonstrate that the company can recover demand without destroying economics. It remains plausible, but the investor should now require the recovery to appear in the same product-volume evidence and in ROE; margin and cash alone are insufficient offsets.

Local UNKNOWNs remain only where they belong: consumer repurchase and price elasticity by channel; channel acquisition/fulfilment economics; the division of cash between working-capital timing, discretionary versus maintenance capex, and business groups; and utilisation, incremental cash, and project returns for individual digital, overseas, or innovation investments. None invalidates the settled company-level demand, product-margin, cash-proxy, or ROE observations, and none is a reason to withhold the revised whole-company judgment.

## Next cutoff research agenda

Remove the FY2024 mechanical questions from the agenda: the three product-volume comparisons, the three product-margin comparisons, the specified cash-proxy calculation, and the weighted-average ROE threshold are answered for this outcome period and should not be re-litigated.

Retain one highest-value FY2025 question: **can liquid milk and cold drinks recover same-product customer volume without surrendering the preserved product margins, sufficiently to restore enterprise ROE to at least the FY2023 level?** The next cutoff should read the same four frozen tables/rows for the new year and use the annual report’s disclosed price/volume bridge if present. A volume recovery that is bought by margin loss, or a margin/cash result that again leaves ROE depressed, does not answer this question affirmatively.

## Method and review adjudication

**No new mandatory frozen field or handling rule is warranted for the next unknown company.** This episode is an instance in which the existing frozen A1–A4 separation worked as intended: cash success did not overwrite customer-absorption or capital-return failure, and two product-margin improvements did not overwrite the cold-drink deterioration. That is correct application of an existing rule, not METHOD_VALIDATED, TRANSFER_VALIDATED, or a new method transfer.

**Defensive-writing finding: none.** The remaining uncertainty is localized and has a bounded investor treatment; it is not used to reject the entire case. Conversely, the review does not disguise the two material failures behind the positive cash result. No CJO, formal valuation, BuyBand, report, or investment authority is granted.
