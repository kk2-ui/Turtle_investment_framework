## Investor consequence relative to the frozen judgment

Before outcome access, the judgment was that a shift toward innovative products was more plausible than a simple generic-model return, but that the transition was not yet proven as a durable cash-funded reinvestment flywheel. The frozen treatment specifically withheld capitalization of the 2023 operating-cash-flow rebound until a further period confirmed the innovative carrier and cash conversion.

All three pre-specified confirmation cells now pass. This is better support for three bounded propositions: (1) the innovative-product revenue carrier continued to grow on the comparable, license-excluded definition; (2) reported profit again converted to operating cash above the contract’s 100% hurdle; and (3) R&D stayed inside the pre-specified revenue-funded range while aggregate revenue grew. The result therefore permits the 2023 collection recovery to be treated as **repeated funding capacity**, and reduces the immediate permanent-loss concern that the transition would require R&D and commercial spending from a weakening cash base.

It does **not** turn operating cash flow into distributable owner cash. The frozen treatment continues to classify R&D as required reinvestment, not surplus, and does not authorize a normalized owner-cash amount. It also does not eliminate the permanent-loss risk from future price/access pressure or a legacy portfolio declining faster than innovative commercialization. No valuation, expected-return, CJO, or investment authority is granted here.

## Residual alternative and next-cutoff agenda

The strongest remaining alternative is that this one-year confirmation combines genuine innovative-product traction with transitory support from collection timing, portfolio/access mix, and licensing-related profit effects rather than demonstrating a self-sustaining, high-return enterprise flywheel. The authorized report itself says that certain up-front out-licensing consideration was recognized as revenue and increased profit; that disclosure does not negate the S2 ratio, but it prevents attributing the pass to recurring product economics or collection discipline alone. S1’s definition properly excludes out-licensing revenue, yet the report still does not establish product-level volume, price, margin, patient demand, or R&D project returns.

At the next annual cutoff, the agenda should shift from asking whether the 2023 recovery merely repeats to testing whether the FY2024 carrier persists under the same comparable innovative-revenue definition, alongside a second-year operating-cash/profit conversion and disclosure on the collection/working-capital driver. The decision-relevant discriminator is whether innovative commercialization and cash conversion remain intact without reliance on non-recurring licensing or timing effects while R&D remains financeable. If they do not, revert to the frozen conservative treatment: treat approvals and pipeline as options, preserve R&D as required cash absorption, and raise the cash-funded-transition component of permanent-loss risk.

## Anti-defensive boundary check

Passing three frozen cells settles only the three predeclared observations. It does not prove that approvals caused sales, that the commercial network caused the revenue change, that management selected a superior R&D portfolio, that every innovative product has durable economics, or that the enterprise is fully understood. Nor does a single correctly settled episode establish `METHOD_VALIDATED` or `TRANSFER_VALIDATED`. The appropriate update is the bounded investor-treatment change above—stronger conditional recovery and repeated funding capacity—while retaining the specified alternative and next observation.
